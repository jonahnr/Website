The free fit check and the paid Analytics Health Check are intentionally separate.

No paid work starts here.

When a paid Health Check is worth scoping

This is for teams that need a clear read on the foundation before committing to a reset, retainer, or intelligence initiative.

01

Dashboards exist, but confidence is low

Leadership has reports, but the numbers still trigger side debates, manual validation, or second spreadsheets.

02

Metrics mean different things in different rooms

Definitions, ownership, and business logic have drifted enough that the same question gets multiple answers.

03

Reporting volume is high, but decisions are slow

The team is busy producing outputs, yet leaders still lack the clarity needed to act quickly.

04

Analytics ownership is unclear

Requests, priorities, and governance move through too many informal channels without a stable senior owner.

Sample 01

Dashboard trust breakdown

Best fit when leaders have reports, but meetings still turn into debates over which number is right.

Recommended path: Decision System Reset

Executive readout

Analytics trust is breaking at the metric definition and ownership layer.

Dashboards are functioning, but the same operating questions are being answered with different filters, date logic, and exclusion rules across teams.

Evidence pattern

  • Three dashboards answer the same weekly performance question.
  • Each dashboard uses a different date basis and customer exclusion rule.
  • No single owner is accountable for maintaining the KPI definition.
Trust breakDefinition drift

Core KPI logic has split across reports.

Decision impactSlow weekly reviews

Leaders reconcile numbers before acting.

Priority fixMetric-to-decision map

Standardize the operating signals first.

Sample 02

Analytics bottleneck diagnosis

Best fit when requests move through one or two people and reporting slows the business down.

Recommended path: Fractional Analytics Consulting

Executive readout

The reporting queue is not just too full. The intake, prioritization, and ownership model is unclear.

The team has the technical ability to build, but there is no stable operating rhythm for deciding what matters, what waits, and who owns the follow-through.

Evidence pattern

  • Urgent requests bypass the backlog and reset priorities weekly.
  • Leadership asks for outputs without a clear decision owner.
  • Manual validation work consumes time that should go to higher-leverage analytics.
Trust breakUnclear intake

Requests are not tied to decision value.

Decision impactAnalytics becomes reactive

The team ships work without a stable operating cadence.

Priority fixSenior ownership rhythm

Install recurring prioritization, standards, and follow-up.

Sample 03

Intelligence readiness review

Best fit when the team wants predictive or automated intelligence, but the foundation needs to be validated first.

Recommended path: Intelligence Lab after foundation cleanup

Executive readout

The use case is strong, but the signal layer is not yet governed enough for reliable intelligence.

The organization has meaningful data, but prediction or automation would amplify existing definition gaps unless the core inputs are stabilized first.

Evidence pattern

  • Candidate signals exist, but ownership and lineage are inconsistent.
  • Outcome definitions differ between operations, success, and leadership.
  • Current reporting lacks a feedback loop for whether recommended actions worked.
Trust breakUngoverned signal layer

Inputs need definition and ownership before modeling.

Decision impactAutomation risk

Bad logic could scale faster than people can catch it.

Priority fixFoundation gate

Stabilize signals, then scope the intelligence build.
